# Zero-tariff trade program expanded in Hainan

It was noon on Dec 18. **At the Wanghao Supermarket in Longhua district, Haikou, Hainan province, 1.5 tons of durians, picked just the day before in Malaysia and sold at nearly half their usual price, were sold out within an hour, reported China Daily.**

The shopping excitement was caused by island-wide special customs operations launched that day in the Hainan Free Trade Port (FTP), the world's largest FTP by area, according to Xinhua. The new rules have increased the share of zero-tariff products in the Hainan FTP from 21 to 74 percent, expanding the list from 1,900 to over 6,600 items, covering home appliances, health products and more.

Hainan's customs system now works with a "first line" and a "second line". According to People's Daily, the first line links Hainan to the rest of the world and allows the easy entry of goods, which can move freely across the island. The second line connects Hainan with China's mainland, allowing efficient control of goods. In this way, both security and openness are ensured.

Travel to Hainan remains as convenient as ever. **Tourists within China do not need to get any extra permits – just bring your ID and you're all set, said People's Daily.** For others, travelers from 86 countries or regions, including Russia, France and Argentina, can enter Hainan without a visa. Delivery services are also not affected.

For Hainan's residents, the plan comes with real-life benefits. Starting Nov 1, locals who have departed the island within a calendar year can buy an unlimited amount of duty-free goods during that year. And this change is less about luxury items and more about saving money on everyday household goods. **"Things like imported baby formula and diapers for my kids – I can now buy them whenever I need them, with immediate purchase and pick-up," Weng Huayu, a mother of two living in Haikou, told China Daily.**

**One of the most interesting courses** for me was introduction to information retrieval. There are many resources available online, but it is often hard to find exactly what you need. In this course, we learned how to use keywords wisely and how to tell reliable information from fake news. For example, instead of writing full sentences, you can use two or three keywords separated by spaces to search more effectively.

To be honest, at first I studied mainly for myself – to read more interesting books and find more reliable information. But as I continued learning, I gradually realized the important role librarians play. Libraries serve everyone, including students, researchers and people who simply want to read for fun. I'm happy to help others discover the joy of reading and the power of information.

# Understanding soap

Although billions of people use soap every day, most of us don't know how it works. As a professor of chemistry, I can explain the science of soap – and why you should listen to your mom when she tells you to wash up.

Water, scientifically written as H2O, is required for all life on our planet. Chemists call molecules that are attracted to water hydrophilic, which means water-loving. These molecules can dissolve in water. So if you were **to wash your hands under a running faucet without using soap**, you'd probably get rid of lots of the hydrophilic bits that are stuck to your skin.

>

On the opposite side are hydrophobic, or water-fearing, molecules, which do not dissolve in water. One example is oil. Oil and water just don't mix, so water alone is not enough to wash off oil completely.

The soap molecule, however, is both. Shaped like a tadpole, it has a round, hydrophilic head and a long, hydrophobic tail. **This not only makes soap slippery, but also gives it the cleaning superpower.**

> 2. 改为否定词提前的倒装句。这个倒装句属于完全倒装还是部分倒装？
>

Think about all the things that make your hands dirty during the day, like food smears, mud, sweat and oils. This is a mix of water-loving and water-fearing materials. Dust and dead skin cells "like" water, while natural oils don't, for example.

When soap molecules come together on your hands, they form a micelle structure, with water-loving heads on the surface and water-fearing tails inside the micelle. This structure traps the dirt, and running water washes it all away.

To get the full effect, wash your hands for at least 20 seconds. Rubbing your hands together helps force the soap molecules into whatever dirt is there to break it up and trap it. Besides dirt, your body is also covered by microorganisms, such as bacteria. Some of them can cause illness and make you smell if you haven't taken a shower in a while. **These microorganisms are protected by a membrane. Soap and water can break this membrane, causing the microorganism to burst open.** The water then washes the remains of the microorganism away, along with the smell.

# Not an easy ride

A heated debate followed a recent Meituan delivery advertisement that showed a female rider named A Lan balancing her passion for photography with her demanding job in Yunnan. The ad was widely criticized for romanticizing the harsh realities of delivery riders' lives and was taken offline within a day.

In the video, A Lan wears a perfect uniform, saves enough money for a 15,000-yuan camera in just three months, and even holds a photo exhibition. **The ad struck many viewers as unrealistic, as it sharply contrasted with the reality faced by China's 10 million delivery riders, who race against tight deadlines, work long hours with little rest, and constantly balance speed and safety.** Growing public awareness of riders' working conditions, fueled by social media, has made people more sensitive to content that ignores these realities.

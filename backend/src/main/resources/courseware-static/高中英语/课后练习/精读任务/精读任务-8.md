# Building culture

When most people think of Lego, they see it as a "children's toy". However, Xu Tianyi, an 18-year-old from Shandong, has turned it into a way to share Chinese culture.

Xu's passion for Lego started young, and by age 10, he became a "MOCer", creating original Lego works without instructions. "MOC" stands for "my own creation".

One of Xu's best-known MOC projects is a Miao headdress made from 3,266 Lego bricks. With few people creating wearable Lego headdresses before, Xu worked out each step by himself. He first designed several basic shapes, selected the best fit by trying them on his head and then added decorations to them. To make it look more like a real silver headdress, Xu also spray-painted every brick.

"There are rich artistic values in traditional Chinese culture. People often think it is too old or far from our daily lives," Xu Tianyi said. "By turning abstract culture into models people can touch, I hope more people can understand and love Chinese culture."

# Holding on once more

A brain-machine interface (BMI) system developed by Neuracle Technology in Shanghai has become the world's first invasive BMI medical device to gain market approval. This means it can be sold legally in China. The product is designed to help patients who have lost their ability to grasp objects due to spinal cord injuries. It marks a big step forward for China's invasive BMI technology, moving from the lab to widespread medical use, reported China Daily.

BMI involves creating a link between the human brain and external devices, enabling control of the body through thoughts in the brain cortex.

A key innovation of the new product is the placement of two electrodes outside the patient's dura mater to detect brain signals amid noises. This method helps prevent damage to brain tissue, as the dura mater is a thick protective layer for the brain and spinal cord. Also, a chip is placed in a small groove in the skull to send signals to the external device. "This way, it will be less noticeable and will look nicer after the operation," explained Zeng Yuhang, an engineer from Neuracle Technology. According to China Daily, the system enables patients to control special gloves with their thoughts to perform activities such as grasping objects.

Additionally, unlike similar technologies abroad, the product is powered wirelessly by a magnetic coil instead of batteries. This way, patients don't need to go through multiple implant surgeries to change batteries.

Clinical trials with 36 participants showed improvements in patients' grasping abilities. One patient, surnamed Dong, regained the skill to pick up a cup, drink water, and set the cup down with the aid of the special glove. To achieve significant results, patients need six hours of daily rehabilitation for over a month, noted The Paper.

# Power under control

When you hear the word "meek", what do you picture? Most of us probably imagine someone who is shy, weak and always gives in to others. In this sense, meekness is seen as a bad thing. But for many of our ancestors, meekness was actually a virtue. How could that be?

Let's look at a modern example. In 2018, mixed martial arts champion Matt Serra was having a meal at a restaurant with his family. At one point, a drunk man came in and started threatening the staff and customers. Serra, a trained champion, could have easily knocked him out. But he didn't. Instead, he calmly held the man down until security arrived.

# Driven by obsession

Moby Dick, by American author Herman Melville, is regarded as one of the greatest sea-themed novels in world literature. It tells the story of Captain Ahab, who is driven by revenge after a white whale named Moby Dick bites off his leg. He forces his crew to join his dangerous hunt for Moby Dick across the oceans. In the end, the ship is destroyed and only one man, the narrator Ishmael, survives to tell the story. Through its powerful symbolism and grand narrative, the novel explores deep ideas like obsession, fate and man's fight against nature.

In this excerpt, Captain Ahab finally emerges from his cabin after the Pequod sets sail. He nails a gold coin to the mast, offering it as reward for sighting Moby Dick. His speech makes the crew excited, while Starbuck, the first mate, considers it crazy.

For three weeks, Ahab kept to his cabin. Then suddenly one day, we looked up and there he was - above us on the quarterdeck. He was a long, lean man, all in black but for the white of his whalebone peg-leg. He had faced into sun and wind for so long that his eyes were screwed into a tangle of creases. The face was as weathered and blank as an ancient statue. ...

Ahab had something in his hand. "Here's a sixteen-dollar piece, look. Mister Starbuck, pass me that mallet!" He placed the huge, glittering coin against the main mast and drove a nail through it. "This is for the man who wins me a certain white whale. You'll know it when you see it. It has a crooked jaw ... He's the beast that took off my leg, and turned me into this stumbling cripple of a half-man. But next time I'll make him pay ..."

It was thrilling talk. We were already looking out to sea, swearing death to the white whale as if it had eaten our own mothers. Sixteen dollars is a year's wage to most men, and each of us was inwardly spending that gold doubloon. I know I was.

Only Starbuck, the first mate, had a look on his face like a thunder cloud.

Ahab saw it. "What's your trouble, churchman? Too risky for you?"

"Oh, I'll risk anything for oil," said Starbuck. "That's my business. Oil. That's what we're here for: to turn a profit, taking whale oil. But revenge? Where's the profit in that?"

Ahab bared his teeth. "Is money all there is to life? Then this planet of ours belongs to the book-keepers, and the stars are only hung up there to be counted and banked! Revenge will make me richer here?" And he banged his chest.

Starbuck seemed more horrified than ever. "Chase a dumb beast to punish it for a crime done out of blind instinct? It's madness!"

Ahab swivelled round on his whalebone leg. He jabbed at Starbuck with the fingers of both hands, his spit flying. "Everything man fears is the colour of that whale! Ghosts, shrouds, masks; things invisible; blind things under the earth. White's a zero. It's a blind man's eye; it's icebergs hanging in the ocean, waiting to claw the hulls out of ships. It's the mystery of Moby Dick that torments me. What face is behind that white mask? We have to smash the mask! We have to know, d'you see?"

The two men stood frozen, their faces so close they must have been breathing each other's breaths. The first mate was very pale. Ahab knew he had won the trial of strength. It was as if Ahab had shaken the very guts out of Starbuck.
